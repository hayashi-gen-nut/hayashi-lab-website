RESEARCH

Research

We investigate steel and composite structures through experiments, numerical analysis, and monitoring, with the aim of improving structural performance and extending service life.

STEEL STRUCTURES

Steel Structures & Bridges

Mechanical behaviour, load-carrying capacity, deterioration, repair, and connections in steel bridges and members

COMPOSITE STRUCTURES

Composite Structures & FRP

Mechanical performance, durability, connections, repair and strengthening of FRP structural systems

EXPERIMENT & ANALYSIS

Experiments & Numerical Analysis

Structural performance assessment combining loading tests, material tests, and finite element analysis

STRUCTURAL HEALTH MONITORING

Structural Health Monitoring

Condition assessment and maintenance technologies using vibration, sensing, image and point-cloud data

LAB CULTURE & DEVELOPMENT

How we develop researchers

We do not select for being “already capable.” We value attitude, integrity, and the willingness to become capable through sustained effort.

01

Try first

Do not decide that something is impossible before trying. Investigate, test, and change the approach when necessary.

02

Develop your own view

We want students to bring their own ideas and aspirations into discussion, not simply wait for instructions.

03

Learn from failure

Failure itself is not the problem. Share it, understand why it happened, and use it to determine the next step.

04

Work with integrity

Deadlines, communication, and respect for other people's time are habits that remain useful far beyond research.

05

Broaden your experience

We encourage conferences, international experience, field visits, collaboration, and interaction beyond the laboratory.

SUPPORT

We support what you want to achieve.

Research topics are guided by available funding, facilities, collaborations, and the laboratory's research direction. Within that framework, we strongly value what each student wants to try, learn, or experience. When an idea is too far from a feasible direction, we discuss it and reshape it together rather than simply rejecting it.

B4 → M1 → M2 → D / DEVELOPMENT PATH

Development from B4 through the Doctoral Course

Students progress in stages from fundamentals to practice, external presentation, and independent research. The schedule below is a standard model and is adjusted through discussion according to the research topic, coursework, practical training, and individual plans.

B4SPRING / APR–AUG

Build the foundations of research

  • Join the laboratory in April
  • Take Bridge Engineering
  • Weekly seminar through Civil and Environmental Engineering Experiment & Exercise I
  • Journal reading and fundamental experiment / analysis training
  • Take English Practice
  • Assist senior students’ experiments and analyses to learn research practice
  • Select a topic related to practical training and begin research gradually with M1 external presentations in mind
  • Join field/factory visits, FEM seminars, and inter-university exchange where useful and agreed
  • Prepare for the graduate-school entrance examination

When substantial laboratory work is requested, payment is provided in principle.

B4SUMMER

Gain hands-on experiment and analysis experience

  • Because many experiments and analyses are carried out in summer, students may be asked to assist after discussing availability
  • Schedules are agreed in advance, including travel and time at home
  • Substantial laboratory work is paid in principle
B4AUTUMN–SPRING / SEP–MAR

Practical training or intensive research

  • International practical training generally starts around September; domestic training around October
  • Before departure, seminars are held about twice a week through Experiment & Exercise II and related activities
  • Journal reading, preparation related to the host organization, experiment / analysis, and English Practice
  • After practical training, laboratory work may be requested in February–March after discussion
  • Graduation trips and other plans are free, but long absences should be discussed early
For students choosing the research-project option

From September to the end of February, students generally work on research on a full-time basis (approximately 8 hours per day, 5 days per week), with weekly individual meetings to review progress. The purpose is concentrated, planned research—not simply being present in the laboratory.

M1SPRING / APR–AUG

Develop research and prepare to present externally

  • Internal progression: continue B4 research and, where appropriate, prepare papers and external presentations from B4 results
  • Students entering from elsewhere: discuss and determine a research topic based on previous experience and interests
  • Weekly laboratory seminar as a course
  • Separate weekly individual research meeting in principle
  • Students interested in overseas research internships discuss host institutions and topics
  • Assist with experiments / analyses where needed; substantial requested work is paid in principle
  • TA duties for relevant courses
M1AUTUMN–SPRING / SEP–MAR

Present research externally and broaden experience

  • Continue research and develop results into domestic/international conference presentations and journal papers where appropriate
  • Weekly individual research meeting in principle
  • Weekly laboratory seminar from September
  • Overseas research internships of roughly 2–3 months for students who choose this path
  • Collaboration, university exchange, and field/factory visits as appropriate
M2APR–FEB

Lead, complete, and hand over your research

  • Plan the work independently, interpret results, and propose the next research steps
  • Master’s research, individual meetings, conference presentations, and journal papers
  • Support junior students and contribute to laboratory activities
  • Complete and revise the thesis, organize data, prepare publications, and hand over research materials
  • Research normally continues through the end of February rather than ending at thesis submission
  • Graduation travel should be planned early around research and revision schedules
B4: Learn & experience→M1: Challenge externally→M2: Lead & leave knowledge→D: Become an independent researcher
DDOCTORAL COURSE

Become an independent researcher, including university and college-of-technology faculty

  • Define research questions independently and plan and execute the research
  • Continuously publish results in international journals
  • Present at domestic and international conferences and expand research networks
  • Gain experience leading collaborative research and mentoring students
  • Develop overseas research activities and international collaboration where appropriate
  • Build a record that supports careers in universities, colleges of technology, and research institutions after the PhD

The doctoral course is the training stage in which this laboratory invests most heavily. To develop researchers, including university and college-of-technology faculty, we prioritize faculty time, research funding, conferences, overseas activities, and collaborative research opportunities as far as possible.

INTERNSHIP

Company internships

We do not actively recommend very short company internships of only a few days because they rarely provide a deep understanding of actual work. Participation is discussed case by case. We place greater emphasis on substantial practical training and choose placements with future career options in mind.

CAREER SUPPORT

Career support

Students can only access part of the information needed to understand a company. When useful, we help gather information through alumni, collaborators, and engineers in industry so that both the student and the employer can make a better, more sustainable match.

FOR INTERNATIONAL APPLICANTS

International applicant policy

Master's applicants: We generally accept overseas Master's applicants through the five-year integrated GIGAKU Innovation Program ↗, with the expectation of continuing through the doctoral stage.

Japanese: High Japanese proficiency is not required at entry, but students are expected to commit to learning enough Japanese for daily communication during the approximately five-year period.

Doctoral applicants: At least three international journal papers are expected as a minimum laboratory target. We place particular emphasis on preparing students for careers as university or college-of-technology faculty and independent researchers after the PhD.

Research topic: Topics are generally proposed by the laboratory based on funded projects, collaborations, facilities, and future research directions, then refined in discussion with the student.

RESEARCH THEMES

Main research themes

Corrosion & repair

Load capacity and repair of steel bridges

FRP & composites

Connections, long-term behavior, repair

High-strength bolts & testing

Joint behavior, slip, bolt force

Analysis & AI

FEM, point clouds, digital technologies

  • Residual strength and repair of corroded steel members
  • Structural use, connections, and durability of GFRP/CFRP
  • High-strength bolted friction joints and thin high-strength steel members
  • Bridge condition assessment using vibration, point clouds, images, and AI

HOW WE WORK

Experiment × Analysis × Field

01

FIELD | Find the problem

Identify research questions from real bridges, construction, and maintenance.

02

EXPERIMENT | Observe behavior

Use loading and material tests to capture structural behavior directly.

03

ANALYSIS | Understand mechanisms

Use FEM and data analysis to explain stress, deformation, and damage.

04

DEVELOP | Advance the research

Connect field, experiment, and analysis through discussion with researchers and engineers.
